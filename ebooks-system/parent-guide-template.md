# JOGA+ Parent Guide Template

> Reusable parent education section for every JOGA+ ebook.
> Tone: supportive, simple, professional, encouraging, easy to understand.
> Parents are partners in the player's development — not coaches, not critics.

---

## Opening Note

You are the most important person in your athlete's development journey. Not their coach. Not their teammates. **You.**

You set the tone. You build the habits. You decide what training looks like at home.

This guide gives you a clear, simple framework to support your athlete — without taking over.

---

## 1. The Parent Role

Your role is to:

- **Create the environment.** Time, space, equipment, consistency.
- **Protect the habit.** Show up for the schedule, not the outcome.
- **Encourage effort, not results.** Effort is repeatable. Results are not.
- **Celebrate the small wins.** A clean rep matters more than a goal scored.
- **Let the coach coach.** Stay in your lane on technique.
- **Be the calm.** Your athlete already feels enough pressure.

You are the foundation. You are not the spotlight.

---

## 2. How Parents Should Support Training

- Have water and snacks ready before sessions.
- Protect training time on the calendar. Treat it like a doctor's appointment.
- Watch sessions when invited. Don't watch when not invited.
- Ask the athlete what they want from you before each session.
- Remove distractions: phones, screens, siblings, chores.
- Cheer effort. Cheer recovery. Cheer the second attempt.
- Drive the athlete to and from training in calm silence if asked.

---

## 3. What Parents Should Avoid

- **Don't coach during drills.** Even if you played at the highest level.
- **Don't correct technique mid-rep.**
- **Don't compare** your athlete to siblings, friends, or other players.
- **Don't replay mistakes** in the car on the way home.
- **Don't bring up performance** at dinner unless the athlete starts the conversation.
- **Don't measure progress in wins.** Measure it in consistency.
- **Don't push past fatigue.** Tired athletes get hurt and quit.

The fastest way to break an athlete's love for their sport is to make every interaction about the sport.

---

## 4. Motivation Tips

- Praise the **process**: *"You showed up. That's what champions do."*
- Praise **effort over outcome**: *"You worked through that even when it got hard."*
- Praise **specifics**: *"Your weak foot looked cleaner today."*
- Avoid generic phrases like *"good job"* — they fade fast.
- Let the athlete **set the goal**, then help them reach it.
- Track wins visually: a calendar with X's, a sticker chart, a streak counter.
- Reward consistency, not performance.

---

## 5. Consistency Habits

The athletes who win long-term are the ones who train when they don't feel like it.

Build these habits at home:

- **Same time, same place.** Training becomes automatic.
- **Pre-pack the bag** the night before.
- **Lay out the gear** in the morning.
- **Track every session** in the progress tracker.
- **Never miss twice.** Missing one day is life. Missing two is a pattern.
- **Build a recovery routine**: sleep, water, food, stretching.

Consistency beats intensity. Every time.

---

## 6. Communication Tips

- Ask **open questions**, not yes/no questions.
- Listen more than you speak.
- Mirror back what the athlete says before responding.
- Don't fix problems they didn't ask you to fix.
- Be aware of tone, not just words.
- Praise privately when meaningful. Praise publicly when earned.
- After a tough session, your first sentence should not be about the sport.

---

## 7. How to Give Feedback

Use the **3:1 ratio**: three pieces of encouragement for every piece of feedback.

When you do give feedback:

- **Wait until they ask** — or until the moment is calm.
- Keep it **specific, short, and behavioral**: *"Your eyes stayed up more today."*
- Never give feedback in front of teammates or coaches.
- Never give feedback in the car immediately after a session unless invited.
- Pair every correction with a question: *"What did you notice?"*

You are not their analyst. You are their safe place.

---

## 8. How to Track Progress

Use the **Progress Tracker** at the back of this guide.

Each week, sit with your athlete for 5 minutes and ask:

1. How many sessions did you complete?
2. What's one thing that got better?
3. What's one thing you want to work on next week?
4. Did you complete the weekly challenge?
5. On a scale of 1–10, how was your week?

Sign the tracker. Take a photo. Build the archive.

Long-term development is invisible week to week, but unmistakable year to year.

---

## 9. How to Help Without Overcoaching

Ask before correcting:

- *"Do you want me to watch, or do you want to train solo?"*
- *"Do you want feedback, or just company?"*
- *"Should I time you, or just hang out?"*

Then **honor the answer.**

If the answer is *"just watch,"* watch in silence. If the answer is *"give me feedback,"* give one note — not five.

The goal is **player independence.** A player who only performs when a parent corrects them will struggle when the parent isn't there.

---

## 10. Sample Parent Questions After Training

Use these instead of *"How did it go?"*:

- What did you learn today?
- What part of the session was the most fun?
- What's one thing you did better than last time?
- What's one thing you want to try next session?
- Who did you encourage today?
- What did your coach say that stuck with you?
- What are you proud of from today?

The right question opens the athlete. The wrong question closes them.

---

## 11. Parent Reminder Section

Read this once a month:

- The goal is **long-term love of the sport**, not short-term performance.
- Your athlete is **a child first**, an athlete second.
- **Burnout** is the #1 reason talented kids quit.
- **Joy** is the #1 reason great athletes keep going.
- The best thing you can give your athlete is **belief**.
- The worst thing you can give your athlete is **pressure disguised as support**.
- You are doing better than you think. Keep going.
